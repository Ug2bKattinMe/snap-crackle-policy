How the coupled model works
  1. A monthly chain for the 20.4 million expansion adults. Each person is in one of 48 combined states: Medicaid, uninsured or other coverage; never on SNAP, on SNAP, recently cut or cut; employed or not; subject to work rules or exempt.
  2. Checks drive Medicaid losses. Each person's first check comes at their next annual renewal, then every six months. Failure rates are set from Nebraska's 2026 results, are higher for adults not employed and for people already cut from SNAP, and lower for exempt adults. People can re-enroll after showing a compliant month. A second run without the new rules is subtracted.
  3. SNAP losses are anchored to the fall observed since July 2025, with the share caused by the law as a range, plus further 2027 losses. Inside the chain, adults not working lose SNAP under the time limit.
  4. Losing SNAP raises the odds of a hospital stay on a logistic scale: an odds ratio for losing participation times an odds ratio per $10 of benefit, converted from 2026 prices to the study's prices, building up over three months.
  5. Jobs and coverage are linked. Job loss and finding rates follow the unemployment path; a hospital stay can lead to job loss; not working raises the chance of failing the Medicaid check; in non-expansion states a job loss can push low-income Marketplace enrollees below 100% of poverty.
  6. Hospital care for the newly uninsured uses ER and stay rates relative to insured adults from 2023–2026 payer data, at 2027 hospital costs; part of the unpaid cost may pass to commercial premiums.
  7. Inputs move together. One shared factor links Medicaid, SNAP and Marketplace losses (state paperwork and verification), another links unemployment, job loss and hiring. 10,000 scenarios; 95% intervals are the 2.5th to 97.5th percentiles.

Methodology

This is a scenario forecast built for this page, not an official projection. Every input range is listed below with its basis.

  1. Start from the people each 2027 rule reaches. Medicaid expansion adults by state (20.4 million, scaled to CMS's June 2025 count), SNAP recipients (FY2025, USDA), immigrants losing Medicaid or Medicare (CBO), Marketplace enrollees and Medicare enrollees, all from the companion enrollment page and 2027 policy report.
  2. Apply loss rates and timing. Each group gets a range for how many lose coverage or benefits and how fast. Medicaid losses build through 2027 as states check work hours and run six-month renewals; SNAP losses are already in place from the November 2025 rule change; Marketplace losses land mostly at the January 1 plan-year start.
  3. Add job losses. Jobs lost from the cuts equal lost federal dollars times jobs per $1 billion from an input-output model (Commonwealth Fund/GWU, IMPLAN). The economy-wide change is the Federal Reserve's unemployment projection applied to the labor force. The larger of the two is counted.
  4. Remove double counting. Adults who lose both Medicaid and SNAP under the same work rules are counted once, as are job losers already in another group.
  5. Turn people into dollars. Federal spending reduction = people losing each program times its average federal cost (Medicaid per expansion adult, SNAP benefit, premium tax credit), over the months they are off. GDP loss applies a spending multiplier to each. State and local tax loss is a share of GDP loss. For hospital and ER care, the people who lose Medicaid, Medicare or Marketplace coverage and stay uninsured are multiplied by ER visit and hospital stay rates and by the hospital cost of each visit and stay (HCUP); the unpaid share of that cost is uncompensated care. New out-of-pocket costs are applied per newly uninsured person-month. Medicare cost increases apply to everyone who pays the higher Part B premium and Part D costs.
  6. Simulate 20,000 scenarios. Each scenario draws every input from its range (uniform or triangular). The headline is the median scenario; the 95% interval is the 2.5th to 97.5th percentile. States get each scenario's national totals in proportion to their enrollment, SNAP, population or Medicare shares, so state intervals move together.
  7. Report at 6 and 12 months. People counts are as of June 30 and December 31, 2027; dollars are totals over January–June and January–December 2027, in 2027 dollars.

Model inputs

Input Low Central High Unit Basis
  • The 95% interval covers uncertainty in these inputs only. It does not cover a recession, new legislation, court rulings, or states delaying work rules under good-faith exemptions, any of which could move results outside it.
  • Loss rates come from early evidence (Arkansas 2018, SNAP caseloads since late 2025) and CBO estimates for the enacted law; real 2027 losses depend on each state's exemptions, data matching and outreach.
  • Job and GDP effects use multipliers from a study of the House-passed bill applied to this forecast's smaller 2027 dollar amounts; most of the law's cuts fall after 2027, so later years would be larger.
  • Dual enrollees keep Medicaid help with Medicare costs and are exempt from work rules; they appear here through the Medicare cost increases, not as coverage losses.
